# 4. Types of Payments

!!! abstract "Learning objective"
    Distinguish retail vs wholesale payments; credit transfers vs Direct Debits; push vs pull payments; single vs bulk/batch payments.

## Core concepts

Retail vs wholesale: Retail payments are typically lower value, high volume (a grocery shop, a salary). Wholesale (high-value) payments are large sums between banks/corporates, often time-critical — settled via RTGS-type systems like CHAPS.

Push vs pull: A push payment is initiated by the payer ("I am sending you money now") — a bank transfer or Faster Payments. A pull payment is initiated by the payee, who "pulls" money with prior authorisation — a Direct Debit.

Credit transfer vs Direct Debit: a credit transfer is push (payer-initiated); a Direct Debit is pull (payee-initiated, with a mandate).

Single vs batch/bulk: a single payment is processed individually, often instantly (Faster Payments); a batch payment groups many transactions and processes them at set times (Bacs runs a 3-day cycle, Lesson 8).

## Visual overview

## Key terms

**Retail payment**
:   Lower-value, high-volume payment, typically consumer-related.

**Wholesale / high-value payment**
:   Larger, often time-critical payment, typically corporate/interbank.

**Push payment**
:   Payment initiated by the payer.

**Pull payment**
:   Payment initiated by the payee using pre-authorised access (e.g. Direct Debit mandate).

**Credit transfer**
:   A push payment moving funds from payer to payee.

**Direct Debit**
:   A pull payment where the payee collects funds from the payer under a signed mandate.

**Batch / bulk processing**
:   Grouping many payments to process together at scheduled times.

## Worked example

!!! example
    A Netflix subscription is collected by Direct Debit (pull) — Netflix requests the money. A salary is paid by Bacs credit transfer (push, batch) — the employer sends it. A £400,000 house completion payment moves via CHAPS (push, wholesale, same-day, high-value).

## Comparison

**Payment type dimensions**

| Dimension | Option A | Option B |
|---|---|---|
| Value / volume | Retail (low value, high volume) | Wholesale (high value, lower volume) |
| Initiator | Push (payer-initiated) | Pull (payee-initiated, mandated) |
| Processing | Single / real-time | Batch / bulk |
| Typical rail | Faster Payments, CHAPS | Bacs (batch), Direct Debit |

## Key points

- Retail payments: high volume, low value, consumer-facing.
- Wholesale payments: lower volume, high value, time-critical, often interbank/corporate.
- Push = payer decides; Pull = payee collects via mandate.
- Batch processing groups transactions; real-time processes immediately, one at a time.

## Exam & interview tips

!!! tip
    - Direct Debit = pull, always payee-initiated with a mandate — one of the most-tested distinctions.
    - CHAPS = wholesale/high value; Bacs = retail/batch; Faster Payments = retail/real-time push.

!!! note "Memory trick"
    Pull = they Pull it from you (Direct Debit); Push = you Push it to them (Credit Transfer).

## Scenario questions

??? question "A gym wants to collect membership fees monthly without asking each time. What mechanism, and why?"
    Direct Debit — a pull payment under a signed mandate collects on schedule without re-authorisation each time.

??? question "A company needs a £2m property purchase completed today with guaranteed same-day, irrevocable settlement. Which system?"
    CHAPS — wholesale, high-value, same-day real-time gross settlement.

??? question "Why might a Direct Debit be easier to dispute than a card payment made directly by the customer?"
    The UK Direct Debit Guarantee gives payers strong rights to an immediate refund for errors, since the payee initiated the withdrawal.

??? question "A payroll department processes 50,000 salaries overnight rather than one at a time. What method, which scheme?"
    Batch/bulk processing, commonly supported by Bacs.

??? question "Explain to a trainee why Faster Payments feels different from a Direct Debit."
    Faster Payments is push — you actively choose to send money each time. Direct Debit is pull — the payee is pre-authorised to withdraw agreed amounts on their own schedule.
