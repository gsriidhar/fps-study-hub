# 5. Payment Instruments Overview

!!! abstract "Learning objective"
    List the main payment instruments used by consumers and businesses; explain the pros/cons of each; understand how instrument choice relates to risk and speed.

## Core concepts

A payment instrument is the tool used to instruct or carry out a payment. Common instruments: cash (instant, no bank involvement, but risky to carry and hard to track); cheques (paper instructions, in steep decline but still used for some UK business/legal payments, cleared via the Image Clearing System); cards (debit/credit, Lessons 17-18); credit transfers (bank-to-bank push instructions — Bacs, Faster Payments, CHAPS, SEPA, wires); Direct Debits (pull instructions under mandate); and digital wallets (Apple Pay, Google Pay, PayPal — usually sit on top of an underlying card or bank transfer rather than being a separate rail).

Each instrument trades off speed, cost, reversibility, and traceability. Cash is instant and anonymous but has no audit trail. Cards are fast and widely accepted but carry interchange fees and fraud/chargeback risk. Credit transfers are efficient for known payees but usually irreversible once sent. Cheques are slow and rare but legally significant for some uses.

## Visual overview

## Key terms

**Payment instrument**
:   The tool/method used to make a payment (cash, card, transfer, cheque, wallet).

**Cheque**
:   A written, signed instruction to a bank to pay a specific amount to a named payee.

**Image Clearing System (ICS)**
:   The UK system that clears cheques using digital images instead of physical paper.

**Digital wallet**
:   An app/service (e.g. Apple Pay, PayPal) that stores payment credentials, usually via an underlying card or account.

**Interchange fee**
:   A fee paid between banks in a card transaction, usually acquirer to issuer (Lesson 18).

## Worked example

!!! example
    UK cheque usage has fallen over 90% since the early 2000s but cheques remain valid for payees who don't take cards; the Image Clearing System lets banks clear a photographed cheque within about 2 hours. In much of Africa and Asia, mobile money wallets (e.g. M-Pesa in Kenya) have leapfrogged cards entirely as the dominant retail instrument.

## Comparison

**Payment instruments**

| Instrument | Speed | Reversible? | Typical use |
|---|---|---|---|
| Cash | Instant | No | Small, in-person, anonymous purchases |
| Cheque | Slow (hours-days via ICS) | Can be stopped before clearing | Legacy business/legal payments |
| Card | Instant at POS | Chargeback possible | Retail purchases, online shopping |
| Credit transfer | Minutes to 3 days | Usually not once sent | Salaries, bills, large purchases |
| Direct Debit | Scheduled, pull | Guaranteed refund rights (UK) | Recurring bills/subscriptions |
| Digital wallet | Instant | Depends on underlying method | Mobile/online/contactless |

## Key points

- Payment instruments differ in speed, cost, reversibility, and audit trail.
- Cheques persist for niche legal/business use despite steep decline.
- Digital wallets typically ride on top of cards or bank accounts.
- Instrument choice affects fraud/chargeback exposure — cards have more consumer protection than credit transfers.

## Exam & interview tips

!!! tip
    - Digital wallets are usually a "wrapper" around an existing card or account, not an independent settlement rail.
    - Be ready to compare instruments on speed, reversibility, and typical use case.

!!! note "Memory trick"
    CCCDW — Cash, Cheque, Card, credit transfer/Direct Debit, Wallet — five instrument families.

## Scenario questions

??? question "A tradesperson insists on being paid by cheque. What are the likely reasons, and how does clearing work now?"
    Distrust of card fees/chargebacks or no card terminal; clears via the Image Clearing System, typically within about 2 hours to next working day.

??? question "A customer disputes a card purchase vs a Faster Payments transfer for the same amount. Which is easier to reverse?"
    The card payment — card schemes offer chargeback rights via the issuer; a received Faster Payments transfer is generally final.

??? question "Why might Direct Debit suit a small business better than manual monthly payments?"
    It automates collection on schedule via mandate, reducing missed/late payments, with a small risk of failed collections under the guarantee scheme.

??? question "A company wants to pay 10,000 suppliers overnight at lowest cost, no urgency. Which instrument fits?"
    Bacs batch credit transfer — cost-efficient for bulk, non-urgent payments on a scheduled cycle.

??? question "Why might regulators worry more about consumer protection in cards than in cash?"
    Cards are electronic, traceable, and reversible under scheme rules; cash has no natural dispute mechanism, so its regulatory concerns focus on money laundering and physical security instead.
