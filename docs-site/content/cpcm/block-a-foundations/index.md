## Revision summary

Payments move money; cash management decides where it should sit. The ecosystem has four layers: end users, institutions/PSPs, schemes/rails, and settlement/regulators. Every payment has participants — payer, payee, their banks, sometimes correspondent banks, and (for cards) issuer/acquirer. Payments can be classified as retail vs wholesale, push vs pull, and single vs batch. Instruments (cash, cheque, card, credit transfer, Direct Debit, wallet) trade off speed, cost, and reversibility.

## Flashcards

??? question "What's the difference between payments and cash management?"
    Payments move money; cash management manages balances/liquidity.

??? question "Name the 4 ecosystem layers."
    End users, institutions/PSPs, schemes/rails, settlement/regulators.

??? question "Issuer vs Acquirer?"
    Issuer = cardholder's bank; Acquirer = merchant's bank.

??? question "Push vs Pull?"
    Push = payer-initiated (credit transfer); Pull = payee-initiated with mandate (Direct Debit).

??? question "What clears UK cheques today?"
    The Image Clearing System (ICS), using digital images.

??? question "What is a correspondent bank?"
    A bank providing account/processing services to another bank, often cross-border.

??? question "Retail vs wholesale payments?"
    Retail = high volume/low value; wholesale = lower volume/high value, time-critical.

??? question "Is a digital wallet a separate settlement rail?"
    No — usually sits on top of an existing card or bank account.

??? question "Who runs Bacs and Faster Payments?"
    Pay.UK is the scheme operator.

??? question "What gives UK Direct Debit payers strong protection?"
    The Direct Debit Guarantee.
