## Revision summary

KYC underpins the whole AML framework, applying due diligence proportionately to risk: Simplified for lower-risk customers/products, Standard CDD for most customers, and Enhanced for higher-risk customers like PEPs or those in high-risk jurisdictions — with ongoing monitoring continuing throughout the relationship, not just at onboarding. Sanctions screening — checking every party in a transaction against lists from bodies like OFAC (US) and OFSI (UK) — carries severe consequences for breaches, given OFAC's particularly broad extraterritorial reach over USD transactions. Operational risk covers failures across people, process, systems, and external events, distinct from credit or market risk, mitigated through segregation of duties, business continuity planning, and automation. Cybersecurity threats — malware, ransomware, DDoS attacks, and data breaches — are countered through PCI DSS compliance and the layered defences of encryption, tokenisation, and multi-factor authentication. Data protection under GDPR/UK GDPR governs how personal data is handled through principles like purpose limitation and data minimisation, granting individuals rights including access, rectification, and erasure, the last of which carries important regulatory exceptions for firms like banks.

## Flashcards

??? question "SDD, CDD, EDD, in order of risk?"
    Simplified (low risk), Standard/CDD (most customers), Enhanced (high risk, e.g. PEPs).

??? question "What is a PEP?"
    A Politically Exposed Person — someone holding a prominent public position, or their close associate/family, carrying elevated risk.

??? question "Name three key sanctions bodies."
    OFAC (US), OFSI (UK), and the UN Security Council.

??? question "What is a 'false positive' in sanctions screening?"
    A flagged match that, on investigation, turns out not to be a genuine sanctioned party.

??? question "What are the four categories of operational risk source?"
    People, Process, Systems, and External events.

??? question "What does segregation of duties prevent?"
    One single person both initiating and approving a critical action, such as a payment.

??? question "What is PCI DSS?"
    The Payment Card Industry Data Security Standard, applying to any organisation handling card data.

??? question "Encryption vs tokenisation?"
    Encryption scrambles data using a cryptographic key; tokenisation replaces the data entirely with a non-sensitive substitute.

??? question "What does GDPR govern?"
    The collection, use, storage, and sharing of personal data.

??? question "Does the right to erasure apply unconditionally?"
    No — it's subject to exceptions, such as regulatory record-keeping requirements.

??? question "What triggers Enhanced Due Diligence?"
    Higher-risk factors like PEP status, a high-risk jurisdiction, or a complex/opaque corporate structure.

??? question "What is ransomware?"
    Malware that encrypts a victim's data or systems and demands payment for their release.
