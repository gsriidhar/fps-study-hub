## Revision summary

Corporate cash management centres on forecasting and the working capital cycle, captured in the cash conversion cycle (DSO + DIO − DPO), which measures how many days cash is genuinely tied up in a company's operations. Liquidity management uses cash pooling — physical (zero-balancing) pooling, which actually sweeps real cash into a master account, and notional pooling, which only nets balances for interest calculation purposes without moving any money — alongside intercompany loans, to optimise how a group's cash is used across many accounts and currencies. Corporate treasury covers cash/liquidity management, funding, FX and interest rate risk management (using tools like forward contracts, natural hedges, and interest rate swaps), and bank relationship management. Corporate banking products extend the relationship further: trade finance (particularly Letters of Credit) reduces risk between unfamiliar trading partners, invoice financing/factoring accelerates cash collection, revolving credit facilities and term loans offer different borrowing structures, and virtual accounts simplify reconciliation. Reconciliation itself — comparing internal records against external ones across bank, intercompany, and nostro accounts — is a core, daily payments operations control that catches errors, fraud, and processing failures through the careful investigation of every exception found.

## Flashcards

??? question "What is the cash conversion cycle formula?"
    DSO + DIO − DPO.

??? question "Physical vs notional pooling?"
    Physical actually moves cash into a master account; notional only nets balances for interest calculation, with no movement.

??? question "What are the core treasury functions?"
    Cash/liquidity management, funding, FX/interest rate risk management, and bank relationship management.

??? question "What does a forward contract do?"
    Locks in an exchange rate for a specified future date.

??? question "What is a natural hedge?"
    Matching revenue and cost currencies to reduce structural FX exposure, without using a financial instrument.

??? question "What is a Letter of Credit?"
    A bank's guarantee of payment to a seller, released once agreed trade documents are presented.

??? question "What is invoice factoring?"
    Selling unpaid invoices to a financier at a discount for immediate cash.

??? question "Revolving credit facility vs term loan?"
    Revolving allows flexible, repeated drawdown and repayment; a term loan is a fixed lump sum repaid on a set schedule.

??? question "What is reconciliation?"
    Comparing internal and external records to confirm they match, and investigating and resolving any differences.

??? question "What is nostro reconciliation specifically?"
    Matching a bank's own nostro account records against the statement its correspondent bank actually sends back.

??? question "What does DPO measure?"
    The average number of days a company itself takes to pay its suppliers.

??? question "What does STP stand for, and why does it matter for reconciliation?"
    Straight-Through Processing — it reduces manual intervention, which directly reduces the number of reconciliation exceptions.
